# Runbook: v1.8 Quick Demo — a general, ROS-free body (servo)

Connect a USB camera and one hobby servo to a Raspberry Pi 5,
with AI inference on a DGX Spark. Start with mock inference and dry-run hardware,
then enable the servo, then replace the mock with Qwen vision inference. **ROS 2
is not required for any of these three servo demonstrations.**

The example separates a decision from the hardware that executes it. The optional
PuppyPi showcase at the end illustrates another body behind the same action contract.

| Piece | Host | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brain | DGX Spark, NVIDIA GB10, ARM64 Linux | Capture observations over HTTP, run inference, decide and confirm actions |
| Seam | DGX ↔ Raspberry Pi over the local network | `raw_zenoh` carries `{action, params}` and returns execution state |
| Body and camera | Raspberry Pi 5, Raspberry Pi OS, USB webcam, GPIO servo | Serve JPEG snapshots and execute actions through `gpio_servo` |

## What you'll build and learn

```text
USB camera (Pi) → HTTP JPEG → DGX observation → AI inference → action decision
                                                               ↓
                                                         confirmation
                                                               ↓
Pi GPIO18 servo ← gpio_servo body ← Zenoh action + state ↔ DGX live loop
```

- **Brain:** the computer running `scripts/run_live.py`; it coordinates the loop.
- **Observation:** one captured camera image, before any interpretation.
- **Inference:** a model's response to that image and a prompt. Mock inference
  supplies a fixed response to test the rest of the system independently.
- **Action:** the existing `gesture_commander` application maps the model's
  `TROT`/`STOP` response to the lowercase `trot`/`stop` capability. Other text falls
  back to `stop`.
- **Motion confirmation:** the live loop requires two consecutive `trot`
  decisions within a time window before sending motion. A `stop` decision needs
  no confirmation; repeated actions are sent only when the action changes.
- **Seam:** Zenoh transports small action messages and state replies. Camera
  images travel separately over HTTP.
- **Body:** the Pi service uses the `gpio_servo` adapter to sweep or stop the servo.
  A servo's `TROT` means a back-and-forth sweep, not walking.

Afterward, you should be able to prepare both hosts, test sensing and actuation
separately, distinguish a decision from confirmed delivery, replace mock inference
with Docker-hosted Qwen, diagnose common failures, and stop the demonstration safely.

**Validation boundary.** The project team's hardware experiments used OpenPAVE
v1.8.4, `raw_zenoh`, `gpio_servo`, DGX Spark/GB10, Pi 5, the NVIDIA image
`nvcr.io/nvidia/vllm:26.03.post1-py3`, and `Qwen/Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct` on port
8001. The instructions match the interfaces in this repository; the camera script's
repository-packaged version still needs a quick Pi hardware startup check, and
the full fresh-setup walkthrough remains outstanding.
Use the fork’s default `main` branch as shown in §3b.

## 1. Safety card (read first)

- Start with **Demo 1 in dry-run** before any intentional servo movement. The
  direct GPIO preflight is deferred until Demo 2 for this reason.
- Keep the horn clear and unloaded; a small paper flag is enough. No linkages,
  mechanical loads, hands near the sweep, or forced/stalled motion.
- A servo can draw enough current to reset the Pi. Use a suitable regulated
  external **5 V** supply when the servo's current requirements exceed the Pi's
  available power. Connect the grounds as described below.
- Stop the DGX live loop with **Ctrl+C** or let its bounded run finish. When it
  believes motion is active, it attempts a shutdown STOP. Confirm the Pi's
  `[gpio_servo] STOP (center + relax)` log and that sweeping has stopped.
- Keep a second DGX terminal ready for the manual STOP in [§4](#4-network-and-terminal-setup).
  A failed network connection can prevent a STOP from arriving. If motion cannot
  be stopped, switch off the servo power supply; do not reach into the sweep or
  rearrange live wires.
- **Stop the brain and send/verify STOP before exiting the body service.** In this
  checkout, closing `seam_cli.py serve` does not itself call the adapter's STOP.
  The live-loop watchdog is on the brain, not an independent Pi-side power cutoff.
- Power off the Pi and disconnect servo power **before changing any wiring**.
  For a normal shutdown, follow [§10](#10-teardown) before removing power.

Keep someone supervising the hardware throughout these demonstrations.
Confirmation and automatic STOP attempts cannot guarantee safety if the network,
software, or power fails.

## 2. Hardware and wiring (power off first)

You need a Pi 5 with a suitable Pi power supply, a USB webcam, and a 3-wire hobby
micro-servo such as an SG90/MG90-class servo rated for 5 V. Verify the servo's
wire colors and supply rating against its own documentation.

The servo's 3-way female connector must **not** be placed across three adjacent
Pi pins. Use individual female jumper leads or carefully release and insulate the
individual contacts, with all power disconnected.

| Servo wire | Pi connection | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Signal (often orange/white) | **GPIO18 — physical pin 12** | GPIO uses BCM numbering; 18 is not physical pin 18 |
| V+ (often red, middle) | **5 V — physical pin 2 or 4**, or external regulated 5 V | Never pin 1 (3.3 V) or a GPIO signal pin |
| Ground (often brown/black) | **GND — physical pin 6** | Common ground with the external supply if used |

With an external supply, connect servo V+ to that supply **instead of** the Pi's
5 V pin. Connect servo ground, external-supply ground, and Pi GND together; do not
join the two positive supply outputs. No series resistor is required on the servo
signal. Check for loose contacts and shorts before applying power.

## 3. Prerequisites and checkout

Use 64-bit Raspberry Pi OS on the Pi and the supported ARM64 Linux/DGX software
stack on the Spark. Both hosts need Python **3.11 or newer**, Git, Bash, `curl`,
and Internet access for installation. Only the DGX needs Docker, a working NVIDIA
driver, and NVIDIA GPU container support. Allow disk space for the container image
and model weights; the first download can take several minutes or longer.

Use a trusted local network with DGX → Pi TCP ports **8090** (camera) and **7447**
(Zenoh) reachable. These example services have no authentication; run one demo
body, without other OpenPAVE action publishers on the same network. The inference
API will be published only on the DGX loopback address.

Commands below are for **Bash**. On the DGX Spark and Raspberry Pi, open a
terminal; if your shell is not Bash, run `bash` first. Each block identifies its
machine; run commands in order and stop on errors. Keep the camera and body
running in separate terminals so their logs and Ctrl+C controls remain available.

### 3a. Install host packages

**DGX — terminal:** install the small host-side toolset. vLLM and its GPU/Python
libraries will run inside Docker, not in this virtual environment.

```bash
# DGX
sudo apt update
sudo apt install -y git python3 python3-venv python3-pip curl ca-certificates iproute2
python3 --version
uname -m
```

**Expected:** Python 3.11+ and `aarch64`. If the OS does not provide that Python
version, prepare a supported OS/Python installation before continuing.

**Raspberry Pi — terminal:** install the same tools plus the Raspberry Pi OS GPIO
libraries. `v4l-utils` helps identify the correct USB-camera device.

```bash
# Raspberry Pi
sudo apt update
sudo apt install -y git python3 python3-venv python3-pip curl ca-certificates iproute2 \
  python3-gpiozero python3-lgpio v4l-utils
python3 --version
uname -m
id -nG
```

**Expected:** Python 3.11+, `aarch64`, and your user has `gpio` and `video` access.
If those groups are missing, on the **Pi** run
`sudo usermod -aG gpio,video "$USER"`, then log out and reconnect before continuing.
Do not use `sudo pip` to install into the system Python.

This moves into the project directory, where the following commands find their

### 3c. Install the Python dependencies

A virtual environment keeps OpenPAVE's Python packages together in the project's
`.venv` folder. Use `.venv/bin/python` for every OpenPAVE command; you do not need
to activate the environment.

[requirements-seam.txt](../../requirements-seam.txt) installs the pinned communication
packages on both machines. Its Device Connect packages require Python 3.11+, even
though these demos use Zenoh. The Pi also needs
[requirements-seam-camera.txt](../../requirements-seam-camera.txt) for OpenCV camera
capture. vLLM runs in Docker later; do not install it into either virtual environment.

**On the DGX Spark — setup terminal:** create the environment and install the packages.

```bash
cd ~/OpenPAVE
python3 -m venv .venv
.venv/bin/python -m pip install -r requirements-seam.txt
.venv/bin/python -m pip check
.venv/bin/python -c 'import zenoh; from pave_runtime.application import create_application_runtime; print(create_application_runtime("gesture_commander").name)'
```

**Expected:** installation succeeds, followed by `No broken requirements found`
and `gesture_commander`. This checks that communication and decision-making code
can load before you start a demo.

**On the Raspberry Pi 5 — setup terminal:** include the GPIO libraries installed
with `apt`, then add the communication and camera packages.

```bash
cd ~/OpenPAVE
python3 -m venv --system-site-packages .venv
.venv/bin/python -m pip install -r requirements-seam.txt -r requirements-seam-camera.txt
.venv/bin/python -m pip check
.venv/bin/python -c 'import zenoh, cv2, gpiozero, lgpio; print("Zenoh, OpenCV and GPIO imports OK; OpenCV", cv2.__version__)'
```

**Expected:** `No broken requirements found` and the imports-OK message with the
OpenCV version. Stop here if an import fails; use [§11](#11-troubleshooting).
The Pi needs `--system-site-packages` so its environment can see the OS GPIO libraries.

## 4. Network and terminal setup

**DGX and Raspberry Pi — separately:** find each host's local-network address.

```bash
# BOTH hosts, separately
hostname -I
ip -brief address
```

**Expected:** each machine prints its network addresses. Select the IPv4 address

### Prepare the terminals

Keep these terminals open so you can see logs and stop each service with Ctrl+C:

| Machine | Terminal | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Raspberry Pi 5 | Camera | Keep the snapshot server running (§5) |
| Raspberry Pi 5 | Body | Keep the dry-run or real servo service running (§6–§8) |
| DGX Spark | Live loop | Run camera checks and the three demos |
| DGX Spark | STOP | Keep manual STOP available throughout the demos |
| DGX Spark | Qwen | Keep the Docker model server running for Demo 3 |

Use the Pi setup terminal for spare Pi checks while the other two Pi terminals
run their services. Commands run on the named machine; a terminal opened over
SSH belongs to the machine you connected to.

**On the DGX Spark — in each new OpenPAVE terminal (live loop and STOP):**
replace `192.168.1.50` below with the Pi's IPv4 address from `hostname -I`.
Use the same address in both terminals.

```bash
cd ~/OpenPAVE
RPI_IP=192.168.1.50
export SEAM_TRANSPORT=raw_zenoh
export ZENOH_CONNECT="tcp/${RPI_IP}:7447"
unset ZENOH_LISTEN ACTION_TARGET
```

`RPI_IP` saves repeated typing of the camera address. `SEAM_TRANSPORT` selects
Zenoh, and `ZENOH_CONNECT` tells OpenPAVE where to send actions and receive replies.
The `unset` command clears settings for other body roles or targets.
**Expected:** no errors. The connection is checked once the Pi body starts in Demo 1.
These settings belong to this terminal; repeat this block in a new terminal.
The Qwen terminal only needs Docker commands, so it does not need these settings.

**On the Raspberry Pi 5 — in each new OpenPAVE terminal:**

```bash
cd ~/OpenPAVE
unset ZENOH_CONNECT ACTION_TARGET
```

This opens the project folder and clears settings intended for a brain connecting
to another body. **Expected:** no errors. Each body-start command below supplies
its own adapter, dry-run mode, and listening address.

**After a reboot:** the checkout, virtual environment, and model cache persist,
but these foreground services do not restart automatically. Repeat §4 (including
checking the Pi's current IP), start the camera in §5, and repeat Demo 1 before
physical movement. For Demo 3, relaunch Qwen using §8b, verify readiness, and
repeat §8c's inference configuration and validation; do not reinstall dependencies.

**On the DGX Spark — STOP terminal prepared above:** this sends STOP through
Zenoh to the running Pi body. Stop any live loop with Ctrl+C first, so it cannot
send a new TROT after your STOP.

```bash
# DGX — manual STOP; body must be running
.venv/bin/python scripts/seam_cli.py send stop
```

**Expected:** `state:` contains `"status": "completed"` and the Pi logs STOP.
`no_reply` is not a confirmed stop; check the Pi physically and switch off servo
power if necessary.

## 5. Hardware bring-up — camera first

Leave servo preflight until after Demo 1. The camera setup does not use GPIO.
Use the reusable [scripts/snap_server.py](../../scripts/snap_server.py) camera
server. It uses the same OpenCV capture sequence as the team’s tested setup; the
repository-packaged version still needs a Pi startup check.

### 5a. Identify the camera and check for an existing server

**On the Raspberry Pi 5 — spare terminal:** plug in the USB webcam, then find
its device path. Checking port 8090 also avoids starting a second camera server.

```bash
# Raspberry Pi
v4l2-ctl --list-devices
ss -ltnp 'sport = :8090'
```

Find your USB webcam's name in `v4l2-ctl --list-devices` and note its associated
`/dev/videoN` paths. A webcam can list more than one node, including metadata-only
nodes; use a video capture node and confirm it supplies a usable image in §5c.
Device numbers can change after reconnecting, rebooting, or adding other video
devices. Repeat this check after those changes; the physical USB port number does
not tell you which `/dev/videoN` to use.

**Expected on first launch:** your webcam is listed with its device paths, and
`ss` shows only a header, with no listener on 8090. If there is an
existing listener, identify its owner (`sudo ss -ltnp 'sport = :8090'` on the Pi if
needed). Reuse it only if it is the intended snapshot server and its image passes
§5c. An HTTP 503 from an existing server is a capture problem, not permission to
launch a duplicate. Stop your old server in its terminal before replacing it;
do not kill an unknown service just to free the port.

### 5b. Start one foreground snapshot server

**On the Raspberry Pi 5 — camera terminal prepared in §4:** start the camera
server and leave this terminal open. `/dev/video0` is a common default; if §5a
shows your webcam's capture device is `/dev/video2`, use `--device /dev/video2`
instead. This makes its images available to the DGX Spark.

```bash
.venv/bin/python scripts/snap_server.py --device /dev/video0
```

**Expected:** `snapshot server ready: /dev/video0 -> 0.0.0.0:8090` (or your selected
path). This confirms the camera opened; check its image in §5c before continuing.
Ctrl+C closes the server and releases the camera.

The script uses exactly the selected device; it does not search for another.
It keeps the tested capture behavior: discard two warm-up reads, capture the
third frame, and encode JPEG at quality **85**, using the camera's default resolution.
A startup error names the device; a capture or encoding failure returns HTTP 503.
Use [§11](#11-troubleshooting) if either happens.

### 5c. Check HTTP and inspect the image

**Raspberry Pi — spare terminal:** verify local HTTP delivery.

```bash
# Raspberry Pi
curl --fail --show-error --max-time 10 -o /dev/null \
  -w 'snapshot HTTP %{http_code}\n' http://127.0.0.1:8090/
```

**DGX — prepared terminal:** verify the network path and save a frame.

```bash
# DGX
mkdir -p .openpave
curl --fail --show-error --max-time 10 "http://${RPI_IP}:8090/" -o .openpave/camera-check.jpg \
  -w 'snapshot HTTP %{http_code}\n'
```

**Expected:** HTTP `200` on both hosts and a nonempty JPEG on the DGX. **HTTP 200
alone does not prove that the image is usable.** Open the saved JPEG in an image
viewer on the DGX desktop, and verify focus, lighting, orientation, and that a
whole hand is large enough to distinguish. For a headless DGX, copy the exact
saved image to your development machine:

```bash
# Local computer — replace the username and example IP with your DGX login details
scp your-dgx-user@192.168.1.60:OpenPAVE/.openpave/camera-check.jpg ./camera-check.jpg
```

This optional copy requires an SSH client on your local computer and working SSH
access to the DGX. **Expected:** `camera-check.jpg` appears in your local current
folder; open it with your usual image viewer. You can also use a browser on the
same network to visit `http://192.168.1.50:8090/`, replacing the example address
with the Pi's actual IP. Refresh while changing the scene to check image freshness.

**Camera checkpoint:** the saved image is clear, current, and shows the whole
hand. Do not continue with a black, stale, blurred, or incorrectly framed image.
If capture fails, stop the camera server, recheck the webcam's capture nodes (§5a),
change the `--device` path, restart §5b, and repeat these checks.

## 6. Demo 1 — mock inference, dry-run servo

This checks camera → observation → deterministic inference → confirmation →
Zenoh delivery without opening GPIO or moving the servo. Docker/Qwen can stay off.

**Raspberry Pi — dedicated body terminal:** start the adapter explicitly in dry-run.

```bash
# Raspberry Pi
SERVO_DRYRUN=1 SERVO_PIN=18 ROBOT_ADAPTER=gpio_servo \
SEAM_TRANSPORT=raw_zenoh ZENOH_LISTEN=tcp/0.0.0.0:7447 \
  .venv/bin/python -u scripts/seam_cli.py serve
```

**Expected:** `[seam:raw_zenoh] body up · adapter=gpio_servo ...` and no movement.
Leave this terminal running beside the camera terminal.
`ROBOT_ADAPTER=gpio_servo` selects the servo body; `SERVO_PIN=18` selects BCM GPIO18.
`SERVO_DRYRUN=1` prevents GPIO access and movement. `SEAM_TRANSPORT=raw_zenoh`
selects Zenoh, and `ZENOH_LISTEN` opens port 7447 for the DGX connection.
These settings apply only to this command.

**DGX — live-loop terminal:** force `TROT` while still capturing real camera
images. `MOCK_INFERENCE_OUTPUT=TROT` supplies a predictable decision without AI.
Two consistent decisions are required before Zenoh sends motion to the body.
This takes observations at least one second apart and ends after about eight
seconds; it does not interpret your hand gesture yet.

```bash
# DGX
MOCK_INFERENCE_OUTPUT=TROT .venv/bin/python scripts/run_live.py \
  --input-url "http://${RPI_IP}:8090/" --transport raw_zenoh --runtime mock \
  --application gesture_commander --confirmations 2 --confirmation-window-ms 10000 \
  --period-seconds 1 --max-seconds 8
```

**Expected DGX output:** one JSON object per line. These are selected fields from
a successful run; actual records also contain `target` and `state`.

```json
{"tick": 0, "decided": "trot", "confirm": 1, "dispatched": null}
{"tick": 1, "decided": "trot", "confirm": 2, "dispatched": "trot"}
{"event": "shutdown_stop", "dispatched": "stop"}
```

On the dispatch record, check **`state.status` is `completed`**; `dispatched` alone
only says a send was attempted. The Pi should log the following (timestamps omitted):

```text
[gpio_servo] TROT (sweep ±40.0° @ 2.0 Hz)
[gpio_servo] STOP (center + relax)
```

**Checkpoint:** no physical movement, successful `trot` delivery, then STOP in the
Pi log when the bounded run ends. The dry-run adapter logs and records actions,
but never opens GPIO. `shutdown_stop` records an attempt; verify the body log and,
for an explicit reply, run the §4 manual STOP command. Fix `watchdog`, `no_reply`,
or missing Pi output using [§11](#11-troubleshooting) before proceeding.

**What you learned:** a model's decision must pass confirmation and then receive a
body reply. Dry-run tests the software path while leaving physical actuation disabled.

## 7. Demo 2 — mock inference, real servo

### 7a. Stop the dry-run body, then preflight GPIO

**DGX:** let Demo 1 finish, run the §4 manual STOP, and verify its `completed`
reply. **Raspberry Pi:** then press Ctrl+C in the dry-run body terminal. Check
that its listener is gone before starting another body:

```bash
# Raspberry Pi — spare/body terminal after Ctrl+C
ss -ltnp 'sport = :7447'
```

**Expected:** no listener. Keep the camera server running.

The following **Pi preflight deliberately moves the servo**, bypassing OpenPAVE.
Only run it after Demo 1 passes, with wiring checked, power applied, and the horn
clear. It verifies GPIO access and basic wiring/power before adding network control.

```bash
# Raspberry Pi — body terminal, body service stopped
.venv/bin/python - <<'PY'
from gpiozero import AngularServo
import time

servo = AngularServo(18, min_angle=-90, max_angle=90)
try:
    for angle in (0, 45, -45, 0):
        servo.angle = angle
        time.sleep(1)
finally:
    servo.detach()
    servo.close()
PY
```

**Expected:** approximately center → +45° → −45° → center, one second at each
position, then release. If it fails, stop here and use the GPIO/power rows in
§11. Power down before correcting wiring. A software PWM warning does not prove
GPIO failed, but check for jitter and unloaded, controlled movement.

### 7b. Start the real body and trigger confirmed motion

**Raspberry Pi — body terminal:** explicitly disable dry-run rather than relying
on the absence of an environment variable.

```bash
# Raspberry Pi — may cause an initial positioning twitch when GPIO is opened
SERVO_DRYRUN=0 SERVO_PIN=18 ROBOT_ADAPTER=gpio_servo \
SEAM_TRANSPORT=raw_zenoh ZENOH_LISTEN=tcp/0.0.0.0:7447 \
  .venv/bin/python -u scripts/seam_cli.py serve
```

**Expected:** the body-up message. If it prints `gpiozero unavailable (...);
running dry`, this is a fallback, **not** a successful real-servo test; correct
the environment or GPIO access first.

**DGX — live-loop terminal:** repeat the deterministic test, now against the real
body. Keep the manual STOP terminal available.

```bash
# DGX
MOCK_INFERENCE_OUTPUT=TROT .venv/bin/python scripts/run_live.py \
  --input-url "http://${RPI_IP}:8090/" --transport raw_zenoh --runtime mock \
  --application gesture_commander --confirmations 2 --confirmation-window-ms 10000 \
  --period-seconds 1 --max-seconds 8
```

**Expected:** the Demo 1 JSON sequence and Pi TROT/STOP logs, now with movement.
The horn does not start a continuous sweep on `confirm: 1`: the first decision
alone is insufficient. `confirm: 2` within the 10-second window dispatches `trot`,
which sweeps about ±40° at 2 Hz until STOP. Repeated `trot` decisions do not resend
the same action. The initial GPIO positioning twitch is separate from this gate.

STOP stops the sweep thread, requests the home angle (default 0°), then immediately
detaches PWM to relax the servo. **Do not depend on an exact physical center:**
this checkout does not wait for the horn to reach center before detaching.

**DGX — after the bounded run:** explicitly verify STOP delivery again.

```bash
# DGX
.venv/bin/python scripts/seam_cli.py send stop
```

**Physical checkpoint:** the sweep starts only after confirmation and is no longer
running after STOP. Require a `completed` state reply and the Pi STOP log. If the
servo is still sweeping, switch off servo power and diagnose before continuing.

**What you learned:** replacing the dry-run body with GPIO changes physical
execution, while the same mock decisions, confirmation gate, and Zenoh messages work.

## 8. Demo 3 — Docker + Qwen inference, then the real servo

Use **`nvcr.io/nvidia/vllm:26.03.post1-py3`** and
**`Qwen/Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct`**, with the host API at
**`http://127.0.0.1:8001/v1`**. The model and image are the project team's tested
combination; see the [NVIDIA image entry](https://catalog.ngc.nvidia.com/orgs/nvidia/containers/vllm?version=26.03.post1-py3)
and [Qwen model card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct).
Docker provides a preconfigured vLLM, PyTorch, and GPU software environment,
without requiring a manual vLLM installation on the host.

First, return to a dry-run body so AI checks cannot move the servo.
**On the DGX Spark:** stop the live loop, then send and verify the §4 manual STOP.
**On the Raspberry Pi 5 — body terminal:** press Ctrl+C to stop the real body,
then check that its port is free:

```bash
ss -ltnp 'sport = :7447'
```

**Expected:** no listener. Now start the dry-run body in the same Pi terminal:

```bash
SERVO_DRYRUN=1 SERVO_PIN=18 ROBOT_ADAPTER=gpio_servo \
SEAM_TRANSPORT=raw_zenoh ZENOH_LISTEN=tcp/0.0.0.0:7447 \
  .venv/bin/python -u scripts/seam_cli.py serve
```

**Expected:** the body-up message and no movement. Keep it dry through §§8c–8d.
Leave the camera running.

### 8a. Verify GPU access inside the pinned image

### 8b. Start Qwen with a persistent model cache

**DGX — Qwen terminal:** check for an existing container or an occupied host port
before starting another server.

```bash
# DGX
sudo docker ps -a --filter name=openpave-qwen
ss -ltnp 'sport = :8001'
```

**Expected for a fresh run:** no container named `openpave-qwen` and no listener.
For an existing container, inspect it with
`sudo docker logs --tail 60 openpave-qwen` and check `/v1/models` below. Reuse a
healthy instance. To replace your running demo container, stop it using §10 first.
If an older, non-`--rm` demo left a stopped container with the same name, remove
only that stopped container with `sudo docker rm openpave-qwen`.

**DGX — Qwen terminal:** create the host cache directory, then launch the validated
configuration. The cache in `~/.cache/huggingface` keeps model files between
runs, so later launches do not download them again. Keep this terminal open for
logs; the first model download needs Internet access and may take several minutes.

```bash
# DGX
mkdir -p "$HOME/.cache/huggingface"
sudo docker run --rm -it \
  --name openpave-qwen \
  --runtime=nvidia --gpus all \
  --ipc=host \
  -p 127.0.0.1:8001:8001 \
  -v "$HOME/.cache/huggingface:/root/.cache/huggingface" \
  --entrypoint vllm \
  nvcr.io/nvidia/vllm:26.03.post1-py3 \
  serve Qwen/Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct \
  --host 0.0.0.0 \
  --port 8001 \
  --dtype bfloat16 \
  --max-model-len 4096 \
  --gpu-memory-utilization 0.55 \
  --enforce-eager
```

**Expected:** download, model-loading, and server-startup messages in this terminal.
These are the project team's validated DGX Spark/GB10 settings. `--rm -it` keeps
the server interactive and automatically removes the container when it exits;
the cached model files stay on the DGX for the next launch. Keep every argument
shown above; these settings are part of the tested configuration.

**On the DGX Spark — live-loop terminal prepared in §4:** once the Qwen terminal
says the server has started, check that the API is ready. Leave Qwen running.

```bash
# DGX
curl --fail --show-error --max-time 10 http://127.0.0.1:8001/v1/models
```

**Expected:** JSON with a `data` entry whose `id` is `Qwen/Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct`.
A connection failure while the model is still loading means it is not ready yet;
wait and run the same check again. First startup can take 15 minutes or longer.
If loading fails or the model ID differs, inspect the Qwen terminal (or, while
the container is running,
`sudo docker logs --tail 100 openpave-qwen`) before continuing. After a failed
`--rm` container exits, use the terminal's error output and relaunch §8b once the
cause is resolved. Image understanding is checked next.

### 8c. Configure OpenPAVE and validate three fixed images (no dispatch)

**DGX — live-loop terminal:** explicitly select Qwen and the API port. Repeat
this block in every DGX terminal used for real inference.

```bash
export INFERENCE_API_BASE=http://127.0.0.1:8001/v1
export INFERENCE_API_KEY=EMPTY
export INFERENCE_MODEL=Qwen/Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct
```

`INFERENCE_API_BASE` selects the local server address, `INFERENCE_MODEL` selects
Qwen, and `INFERENCE_API_KEY=EMPTY` supplies a dummy client key for this local server.
The commands below select `vllm_openai` and `gesture_commander` directly: the first
sends the image and prompt to the API, and the second turns its answer into an action.
**Expected:** no output from these `export` commands. The image checks below
confirm that the configuration works.

The separate [robot-commander-qwen.json](../../prompts/robot-commander-qwen.json)
preset retains the original output contract and safety metadata. The original
[robot-commander-gesture.json](../../prompts/robot-commander-gesture.json) is unchanged.
Select the new preset explicitly using `--prompt-preset`. Its prompt is:

> Identify the hand gesture in this image. If it is a thumbs-up gesture, respond with TROT. For any other gesture or if you are uncertain, respond with STOP. Your entire response must be exactly one word: TROT or STOP.

The application enforces parsing and the live loop enforces motion confirmation;
the prompt alone cannot provide either guarantee.

**On the DGX Spark — same live-loop terminal:** save three images, one at a time.
These fixed inputs let you check Qwen's decisions without sending any actions.

Finally, remove **all hands** from the scene, let the image settle, then run:

```bash
curl --fail --show-error --max-time 10 "http://${RPI_IP}:8090/" \
```

**Expected after each capture:** `curl` finishes without an error and saves the

### 8d. Run real inference with the dry-run body

Confirm that the Pi body currently uses `SERVO_DRYRUN=1`. Point the camera at the
operator; begin with an open palm or no hand.

**DGX — configured inference terminal:** select the Qwen prompt and run the live
pipeline for about 30 seconds. It takes observations at least two seconds apart;
hold each gesture steady so the model can see it. These are the tested settings.

```bash
# DGX — Pi body MUST be in dry-run for this first Qwen live run
.venv/bin/python scripts/run_live.py \
  --input-url "http://${RPI_IP}:8090/" --transport raw_zenoh --runtime vllm_openai \
  --application gesture_commander --prompt-preset prompts/robot-commander-qwen.json \
  --confirmations 2 --confirmation-window-ms 10000 \
  --period-seconds 2 --max-seconds 30
```

**Expected:** `decided: stop` initially, with the first STOP dispatched. Hold a
clear thumbs-up steady for two consecutive observations within 10 seconds:
`decided: trot`, `confirm: 1`, then `confirm: 2` and `dispatched: trot`, with
`state.status: completed`. Open palm or no hand should lead to `decided: stop`
and a STOP dispatch if the previous action was different. The Pi logs TROT/STOP,
and **nothing physically moves** in this stage. Repeated STOP decisions may show
`dispatched: null` because the body was already told to stop.

**Dry-run checkpoint:** all three gestures produce the expected decisions,
dispatched actions receive `completed` replies, and nothing moves. If the run
finishes before you try every gesture, repeat the same command while the body is
still dry. Pass this check and all three fixed-image checks before §8e.

These are tested demonstration settings, not universal performance recommendations.
Inference and seam time contribute to the tick interval; the observed approximately
**five-second gesture-to-servo response** was not a formal latency benchmark.

The watchdog allows up to 4000 ms (4 seconds) for each camera capture and each
inference wait. It attempts STOP on a timeout/error while motion is active
(`event: watchdog`). Ctrl+C and the end of a bounded run also attempt STOP for
active motion; verify delivery on the Pi.
The period and run limit are checked between ticks, so neither is an exact timing
guarantee. A timeout is not a hard four-second end-to-end stopping guarantee.

### 8e. Enable physical movement only after both AI checks pass

**DGX:** stop the dry-run loop and send/verify the §4 manual STOP. **Pi:** Ctrl+C
the dry-run body, check that port 7447 is free, and restart the **Demo 2 real-body
command** with `SERVO_DRYRUN=0`. Leave the camera and Qwen container running.

**DGX:** repeat the exact §8d live-loop command. Begin with open palm/no hand;
then hold thumbs-up until confirmed, and return to open palm to stop. Keep the
horn clear and the spare STOP terminal ready.

**Physical checkpoint:** confirmed thumbs-up produces a sweep; open palm/no hand
produces STOP and the sweep ceases. If the actual behavior differs, Ctrl+C,
send/verify STOP, and return to dry-run diagnosis. Do not reduce confirmation
requirements or extend watchdogs to mask a failure. Finish by sending the manual
STOP and checking its `completed` reply and the Pi's stopped state.

The fixed-image checks never dispatch actions. A separate single-shot mode,
`run_inference.py --seam`, requires explicit motion permission and uses a timed
motion lease; it is not used here. The live loop instead handles confirmations,
the watchdog, and shutdown STOP. Neither mode guarantees delivery of STOP if the
network fails or the process is lost.

**What you learned:** Docker hosts the vision model, the prompt constrains its
response, and the existing application converts it to an action. Fixed-image
validation and a live dry-run establish separate checkpoints before physical
actuation; confirmations and verified stopping remain necessary with real AI.

## 10. Teardown

Follow this order while the network and Pi body are still available.

1. **DGX — live-loop terminal:** press Ctrl+C, or let the bounded run finish.
   Confirm the Pi STOP log when motion was active.
2. **DGX — STOP terminal prepared in §4:** send an explicit STOP and verify the reply.

   ```bash
   # DGX
   .venv/bin/python scripts/seam_cli.py send stop
   ```

   Require `state.status: completed`, `[gpio_servo] STOP (center + relax)` on the
   Pi, and no ongoing sweep. If a STOP is unconfirmed, treat the physical state as
   unknown; switch off servo power before troubleshooting if necessary.
3. **Raspberry Pi — body terminal:** now press Ctrl+C to close the body service.
   **Pi camera terminal:** press Ctrl+C to release the webcam and HTTP port.
   These foreground processes need no broad process-kill commands.
4. **DGX — terminal:** gracefully stop only this demo's Qwen container.

   ```bash
   # DGX — if Demo 3 started this container
   sudo docker stop --time 30 openpave-qwen
   sudo docker ps -a --filter name=openpave-qwen
   ```

   Expect the container to exit and disappear because it was launched with
   `--rm`. This releases GPU resources and preserves
   `$HOME/.cache/huggingface`. To run Qwen again, repeat the §8b `docker run`
   command and readiness check; there is no stopped container to restart.
5. **Raspberry Pi — spare terminal:** check both service ports and, if ending the
   hardware session, shut down the Pi normally.

   ```bash
   # Raspberry Pi
   ss -ltnp 'sport = :7447 or sport = :8090'
   ```

   **Expected:** no demo listeners. If the hardware session is finished, shut down
   the Pi from this same terminal:

   ```bash
   sudo shutdown -h now
   ```

   **Expected:** the Pi shuts down and any SSH session disconnects. Wait for
   shutdown to finish, then disconnect Pi and external servo power before touching
   wiring.

If a dedicated camera terminal was lost, **on the Pi** inspect
`pgrep -af 'scripts/snap_server.py'` and `sudo ss -ltnp 'sport = :8090'`, identify
your exact snapshot-server PID, then use `kill -TERM PID` with that numeric PID.
The snapshot server handles SIGTERM by closing HTTP and releasing the camera.
For a lost body terminal, send/verify STOP first, identify the exact `scripts/seam_cli.py serve`
process with `pgrep -af 'scripts/seam_cli.py serve'`, then terminate that PID. Never
use broad `pkill -9` patterns as a routine shutdown method.

## 11. Troubleshooting

Use the checks on the named machine. These recovery steps have not all been
hardware-tested. After any change, repeat the relevant dry-run checkpoint before
enabling motion.

| Symptom | Check and recovery |
| --- | --- |
| Missing OpenPAVE checkout / `cd` fails | **Either host:** this guide expects `~/OpenPAVE` on that machine. Use §3b to clone the fork into your home folder. A checkout on your local computer does not also exist on the DGX or Pi. |
| `can't open file scripts/...` or prompt not found | **Either host:** `cd ~/OpenPAVE`; `pwd` and `ls scripts/run_live.py prompts/robot-commander-qwen.json` should show the expected checkout/files. If a required file is missing from `main`, stop and report it to the maintainer. |
| `.venv/bin/python` not found | **Either host:** run `cd ~/OpenPAVE`, then `ls .venv/bin/python`. If it is missing, create the environment and install requirements using §3c on that machine. |
| `ModuleNotFoundError` for `zenoh`, `cv2`, or GPIO libraries | **Either host:** use `.venv/bin/python -m pip check` and the §3c imports. Install `requirements-seam.txt`; install `requirements-seam-camera.txt` only on the Pi. **Pi:** verify `python3-gpiozero`, `python3-lgpio` and `include-system-site-packages = true`; use this venv's Python, not another interpreter. |
| DGX cannot reach Pi / wrong IP | **Pi:** rerun `hostname -I` / `ip -brief address`. **DGX:** repeat the §4 DGX terminal block with the current `RPI_IP`; this also updates `ZENOH_CONNECT`. Check the shared network and rules allowing DGX → Pi TCP 8090 and 7447. A failed ping alone is not proof these TCP ports are blocked. |
| Camera connection refused / server not running | **Pi:** inspect the camera terminal and `ss -ltnp 'sport = :8090'`; start §5b if absent. Local curl must succeed before DGX curl. **DGX:** repeat §5c. |
| Port 8090 already occupied / `Address already in use` | **Pi:** `sudo ss -ltnp 'sport = :8090'` identifies the listener. Inspect its image before deciding to reuse it. Stop only your known old snapshot server via its terminal or identified PID (§10); do not start a duplicate or terminate an unrelated service. |
| Camera startup failure or HTTP 503 | **Pi:** read the server terminal's error naming the selected device. Use `v4l2-ctl --list-devices` to match the webcam to a capture node; `--device /dev/video2` selects that exact path, while the default is `/dev/video0`. Some listed nodes provide metadata rather than images. Check `video` group membership and whether another application owns the webcam. A JPEG encoding failure also returns 503. Stop a conflicting application if appropriate; after reconnecting, recheck the path and restart the server. Repeat §5c before continuing. |
| HTTP 200 but black, blurred, stale, or unusable image | **DGX/Pi:** inspect saved frames (§5c); uncover/reframe the lens, improve lighting, and verify the intended capture node. Refresh while changing the scene to test freshness. A JPEG can be valid while containing no usable gesture evidence. Keep the body dry until the fixed-image tests pass. |
| vLLM endpoint unavailable / connection refused | **DGX:** check `/v1/models` as in §8b, `sudo docker ps -a --filter name=openpave-qwen`, and the Qwen terminal's error output. While running, `sudo docker logs --tail 100 openpave-qwen` also works; after exit, `--rm` has removed the container and its Docker logs. Fix the cause and relaunch §8b. Ensure `INFERENCE_API_BASE` uses **8001/v1**. |
| Docker GPU failure / incompatible driver / pull failure | **DGX:** repeat host and container GPU checks (§§3d, 8a), and consult the linked NVIDIA driver/container prerequisites. For registry/network errors, verify Internet access and your registry credentials. For initialization or memory errors, inspect logs, `nvidia-smi`, and `sudo docker ps`; stop a known old demo container gracefully. Shared-memory/GPU capacity and driver compatibility require verification on your DGX; do not kill unrelated GPU jobs. |
| Model returns STOP despite thumbs-up | **DGX:** inspect the exact saved image and `inference.text` in the §8c JSON. Verify `INFERENCE_MODEL`, `/v1/models`, and `--prompt-preset prompts/robot-commander-qwen.json`. Improve lighting/framing. Extra prose or punctuation causes the application's STOP fallback. Uncertain images should produce STOP, but misclassification is still possible. `run_live.py` does not print raw model text; use the inference-only fixed-image check, without diagnostic code changes. |
| `confirm` remains 1 / no TROT dispatch | **DGX:** use two consecutive `trot` decisions within `--confirmation-window-ms 10000`. STOP, errors, or an expired window reset confirmation. The original default window is only 1500 ms, shorter than this guide's 2-second AI tick period. Check inference time and hold the gesture steadily; do not remove confirmations. A completed `trot` with later `dispatched: null` is normal edge-triggering. |
| `event: watchdog` | **DGX:** distinguish camera failure from inference failure using local/remote snapshot curl and `/v1/models` plus the fixed-image check. The unchanged live watchdog defaults to 4000 ms per capture/infer wait; a first inference may need warmup (§8c). Resolve timeouts while the body is dry rather than weakening safety settings. |
| `state.status: no_reply`, failed state, or no Pi logs | **Pi:** confirm one body is running and listening on 7447 with the intended adapter. **DGX:** check `ZENOH_CONNECT`, network access, and explicit STOP delivery. `dispatched` or `shutdown_stop` alone is not proof of execution. Do not enable physical motion until replies are `completed`. |
| Software PWM / `PWMSoftwareFallback` warning | **Pi:** this reports a software-timed PWM backend. Rerun the unloaded preflight and inspect jitter and stopping behavior; a warning alone does not prove success or failure. [GPIO Zero's servo documentation](https://gpiozero.readthedocs.io/en/stable/api_output.html#servo) discusses timing and jitter, but no alternative backend was validated here. Do not install/configure a different pin factory as an assumed Pi 5 fix. |
| Servo does not move / body says `running dry` | **Pi:** verify `SERVO_DRYRUN=0` and the correct Python/GPIO imports and group permissions. The adapter falls back to dry-run if GPIO construction fails. Stop services and power down before checking signal → physical 12, 5 V → 2/4 (or external), ground → 6. Repeat the direct preflight only after correcting the cause. |
| Servo jitters, stalls, or Pi reboots | **Pi:** stop the run, switch off servo power, and remove mechanical load. Check servo rating, contacts, and current requirements. With all power off, use an adequate regulated external 5 V supply if necessary, with common ground and positive supplies kept separate (§2). Retest unloaded; do not hold a stalled horn against the motor. |
